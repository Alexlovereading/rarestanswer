Rarest answers on an animal prompt are specific animals that still fit the question. The first name in your mouth is usually the one every other player also has, and that name sits in a low tier. The skill is to name a real member of the set that is further down the list, then stop before you leave the set.

The published example is "Name a species of great ape." Open it from [animal trivia](/trivia/animals/) or from the ranked page at [great ape species](/answers/great-ape-species/). Broader technique, including why the first name fails, is on the [finding guide](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/). This page stays on animals, and it does not invent answer keys for breeds, birds, or fish that are not in the published file.

## Which eight names does the sample accept?

The file stores eight answers, rarest first. Tapanuli orangutan is tier 6, Legendary, 100 points. Sumatran orangutan is tier 5, Epic, 70. Eastern gorilla is tier 4, Rare, 40. Western gorilla and Bornean orangutan are both tier 3, Uncommon, 20. Chimpanzee is tier 2, Familiar, 10. Bonobo and Human are tier 1, Common, 5.

Those tiers are what the game scores for this prompt. The source line is Wikidata plus Wikipedia pageviews (2026-10), the 12-month window ending October 2026. The view counts on the answer page are that sum, not a poll of players and not a single month. The [answer hub](/rarest-answers/) lists the other animal prompts. This guide ranks only the great-ape list.

Wikipedia's [Hominidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominidae) article describes the same eight living species in four genera: Pongo, the Bornean, Sumatran, and Tapanuli orangutans; Gorilla, the eastern and western gorillas; Pan, the chimpanzee and the bonobo; and Homo, of which only modern humans remain. The sample list matches that living set. It does not include gibbons. The same article calls a hominoid an ape in the wider superfamily, gibbons included, and a hominid a great ape. If a future prompt says "ape" rather than "great ape," gibbons may belong. On this prompt they are not in the file, so typing gibbon scores nothing.

## Why does the place name beat the headline animal?

People say orangutan, gorilla, chimp, or human. Human is the common row because the list includes our own species and because that is the name a room reaches for. "Orangutan" and "gorilla" feel specific and are still too coarse for this file. The points sit on the species, and the species are separated by place or by a second common name.

The [Tapanuli orangutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapanuli_orangutan) article says the species was described in 2017, is restricted to South Tapanuli on Sumatra, and is one of three living orangutan species, with the Sumatran orangutan farther northwest and the Bornean orangutan on Borneo. The conservation section says that with fewer than 800 individuals in about 1,000 square kilometres, it is the rarest great ape, and that the IUCN lists it as critically endangered. A December 2025 report cited on that page estimated that floods and landslides killed between 6.2% and 10.5% of the remaining population. Those are field facts. They explain why the animal is unfamous. They are not a substitute for the pageview rank that put tier 6 on this file.

Use the pattern without pretending every animal prompt has a 2017 species hiding in it. When the question allows a species, prefer the species with a place, a year, or a second word. Western versus eastern. Bornean versus Sumatran versus Tapanuli. Bonobo versus chimpanzee. The plain genus word is the one the clock will tempt you to type.

## Which extra names does the sample already accept?

Each row stores aliases. You do not need the display name letter for letter. On this file, Tapanuli orangutan accepts "Pongo tapanuliensis." Eastern gorilla accepts "Gorilla beringei." Sumatran orangutan accepts "Pongo abelii." Bonobo accepts "pygmy chimpanzee," "dwarf chimpanzee," "gracile chimpanzee," and "Pan paniscus." Bornean orangutan accepts "Pongo pygmaeus." Western gorilla accepts "Gorilla gorilla." Chimpanzee accepts "chimp," "chimps," "common chimpanzee," "robust chimpanzee," and "Pan troglodytes." Human accepts "Homo sapiens," "humans," "human being," and "modern human." "Grauer's gorilla," "tapanuli" alone, and "western lowland gorilla" are not stored.

Capitalization, accents, punctuation, and a leading "the" are not supposed to turn a good name into a miss. On a name of six letters or more, one wrong character can still match. A different animal cannot. Matching is not a hint to get cute. "Sea monkey" logic belongs to a different game's autocorrect warning, described on the [Krillion page](/games/krillion/). Here, type the animal you mean.

A repeated guess is rejected and does not take another second off the clock. That matters when you meant Sumatran and typed a near neighbor. Correct it if time remains. Do not hammer the same spelling.

## What should you refuse, even if it is a real animal?

Anything outside the eight. Gibbons are apes and are not on this list. Fossil humans are not on this list. "Monkey" is the wrong group. A zoo nickname, a character, and a subspecies you hope is close enough are misses unless the alias is stored. The reveal will show the rarest names and the most common names after you submit. Read it. Then play a different prompt. Memorizing these eight will not tell you the rarest dog breed, the rarest bird order, or the rarest fish, because those lists are not this list.

Other animal prompts on this site rank names inside each prompt by how often their articles are read, not by how endangered the animal is. An endangered animal with a famous article can still be a common guess. An ordinary breed nobody writes about can score higher. This list's Legendary row happens to be the ape Wikipedia calls rarest. Treat that as a coincidence you can learn from, not as a rule that IUCN status equals tier 6.

## How do you practice without spending the daily?

Play the daily once from the [homepage](/). Then use [unlimited mode](/unlimited/), which deals a fresh set and does not move the daily streak. Animal trivia stays on animals, so a sports prompt does not break the drill. Turn the timer off while you are learning the split between genus and species. Turn it on when you want the same pressure as the daily.

Spotlight doubles one scored prompt per round. On this sample, doubling Human turns 5 into 10. Doubling Tapanuli orangutan turns 100 into 200. The boost is spent only when you actually score, so a miss does not burn it. Hold it for the prompt where you have a specific species, not for the prompt where you are about to type the poster animal. [How to find a rare answer](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/) covers that choice across a whole round.

After the prompt, believe the common column. If Human, chimpanzee, and a plain gorilla word are sitting there, you have seen the trap. The next animal prompt will have a different trap. The habit is the part you can carry.
