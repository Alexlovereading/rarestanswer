Rarest answers on an animal prompt are specific animals that still fit the question. The highest pageview total in the list sits in a low tier. The skill is to name a real member further down that pageview list, then stop before you leave the set. The rank is not a poll of players.

The published example is "Name a species of great ape." Open it from [animal trivia](/trivia/animals/). If the line you pasted was "name a rare species of great ape," the pageview order for the [rarest species of great ape](/answers/great-ape-species/) is the list below. Broader technique is on [finding rare answers](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/). This page stays on animals. It does not invent a key for a list that is not in the file.

## Which eight names does the sample accept?

The file stores eight answers, rarest first. Tapanuli orangutan is tier 6, Legendary, 100 points. Sumatran orangutan is tier 5, Epic, 70. Eastern gorilla is tier 4, Rare, 40. Western gorilla and Bornean orangutan are both tier 3, Uncommon, 20. Chimpanzee is tier 2, Familiar, 10. Bonobo and Human are tier 1, Common, 5.

Those tiers are what the game scores for this prompt. The source line is Wikidata plus Wikipedia pageviews (2026-10), the 12-month window ending October 2026. The view counts on the answer page are that sum, not a poll of players and not a single month. Krillion's acceptance and scores may differ. The [answer hub](/rarest-answers/) lists the other animal prompts. This guide ranks only the great-ape list.

Wikipedia's [Hominidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominidae) article describes the same eight living species in four genera: Pongo, the Bornean, Sumatran, and Tapanuli orangutans; Gorilla, the eastern and western gorillas; Pan, the chimpanzee and the bonobo; and Homo, of which only modern humans remain. The sample list matches that living set. It does not include gibbons. The same article calls a hominoid an ape in the wider superfamily, gibbons included, and a hominid a great ape. On this prompt, typing gibbon scores nothing. Gibbons have their own file: the least-viewed stored name is the western grey gibbon, on [gibbon species](/answers/gibbon-species/). A separate prompt, "Name a species of ape," includes gibbons and also fossil apes. Its least-viewed stored name is *Lufengpithecus hudienensis*, on [ape species](/answers/ape-species/). That name does not score on the great-ape prompt.

## Why does the place name beat the headline animal?

Human is the common row because that article has the most pageviews on this list, not because a room was polled. "Orangutan" and "gorilla" are still too coarse for this file. The points sit on the species, and the species are separated by place or by a second common name.

The [Tapanuli orangutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapanuli_orangutan) article says the species was described in 2017, is restricted to South Tapanuli on Sumatra, and is one of three living orangutan species, with the Sumatran orangutan farther northwest and the Bornean orangutan on Borneo. The conservation section says that with fewer than 800 individuals in about 1,000 square kilometres, it is the rarest great ape, and that the IUCN lists it as critically endangered. A December 2025 report cited on that page estimated that floods and landslides killed between 6.2% and 10.5% of the remaining population. Those are field facts. They are not a substitute for the pageview rank that put tier 6 on this file.

Use the pattern without pretending every animal prompt has a 2017 species hiding in it. When the question allows a species, prefer the species with a place, a year, or a second word. Western versus eastern. Bornean versus Sumatran versus Tapanuli. Bonobo versus chimpanzee. The plain genus word is the one the clock will tempt you to type.

## Which extra names does the sample already accept?

Each row stores aliases. You do not need the display name letter for letter. On this file, Tapanuli orangutan accepts "Pongo tapanuliensis." Eastern gorilla's aliases include "Gorilla beringei," "Grauer's gorilla," "Eastern lowland gorilla," and "Mountain gorilla." Sumatran orangutan accepts "Pongo abelii." Bonobo accepts "pygmy chimpanzee," "dwarf chimpanzee," "gracile chimpanzee," and "Pan paniscus." Bornean orangutan accepts "Pongo pygmaeus." Western gorilla's aliases include "Gorilla gorilla," "Western lowland gorilla," and "Cross River gorilla." Chimpanzee's aliases include "chimp," "chimps," "common chimpanzee," "robust chimpanzee," "Pan troglodytes," "Central chimpanzee," "Eastern chimpanzee," "Western chimpanzee," and "Nigeria-Cameroon chimpanzee." Human's aliases include "Homo sapiens," "humans," "human being," "modern human," and "people." Those subspecies spellings score the species row, not a tier above it. "Tapanuli" alone is not stored.

Capitalization, accents, punctuation, and a leading "the" are not supposed to turn a good name into a miss. On a name of six letters or more, one wrong character can still match. A different animal cannot. Matching is not a hint to get cute. "Sea monkey" logic belongs to a different game's autocorrect warning, described on the [Krillion page](/games/krillion/). Here, type the animal you mean.

A repeated guess is rejected and does not take another second off the clock. That matters when you meant Sumatran and typed a near neighbor. Correct it if time remains. Do not hammer the same spelling.

## What should you refuse, even if it is a real animal?

Anything outside the eight. Gibbons are apes and are not on this list. Fossil humans are not on this list. "Monkey" is the wrong group. A zoo nickname or a character is a miss. A subspecies scores only when it is stored, and then it scores the species tier. The reveal will show the rarest names and the most common names after you submit. Read it. Then play a different prompt. Memorizing these eight will not name the [rarest dog breed](/answers/dog-breeds/) on this site. That file's least-viewed stored breed is Segugio Cravin, and German Shepherd is Common. It will also not name a bird order. On [bird orders](/answers/bird-orders/), the least-viewed stored order is Pteroclidiformes, and Passerine is Common. Those lists are not this list.

Other animal prompts on this site rank names inside each prompt by how often their articles are read, not by how endangered the animal is. An endangered animal with a famous article can still be a common guess. An ordinary breed nobody writes about can score higher. This list's Legendary row happens to be the ape Wikipedia calls rarest. Treat that as a coincidence you can learn from, not as a rule that IUCN status equals tier 6.

## How do you practice without spending the daily?

Play the daily once from the [homepage](/). Then use [unlimited mode](/unlimited/), which deals a fresh set and does not move the daily streak. Animal trivia stays on animals, so a sports prompt does not break the drill. Turn the timer off while you are learning the split between genus and species. Turn it on when you want the same pressure as the daily.

Spotlight doubles one scored prompt per round. On this sample, doubling Human turns 5 into 10. Doubling Tapanuli orangutan turns 100 into 200. The boost is spent only when you actually score, so a miss does not burn it. Hold it for the prompt where you have a specific species, not for the prompt where you are about to type the poster animal. [How to find a rare answer](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/) covers that choice across a whole round.

After the prompt, believe the common column. If Human, chimpanzee, and a plain gorilla word are sitting there, you have seen the trap. The next animal prompt will have a different trap. The habit is the part you can carry.
