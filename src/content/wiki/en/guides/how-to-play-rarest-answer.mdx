This page is how to play the Rarest Answer game: what a round contains, what a guess has to match, what the six tiers pay, and what the clock, Spotlight, and unlimited play change. Start today's round on the [homepage](/). A less-viewed legal name scores more. Choosing that name is [finding rare answers](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/).

## What does the game accept as an answer?

Each turn shows one prompt, such as "Name a species of great ape." You type one name. It counts when it matches that prompt's list, including a stored alias.

Matching ignores capitalization, accents, and punctuation. A leading "a," "an," or "the" is dropped when another word remains. On a name of at least six characters, one wrong character still matches, and only when that typo fits a single answer. If two answers are one character away, the guess misses. A different name does not match.

An empty submit does nothing. Typing a guess you already tried, or another spelling of an answer you already hit, is rejected and does not take a second off the clock.

An accepted name already has a tier from 1 to 6 inside that prompt. The tier is not a grade of how clever the sentence sounded. Points come from the tier, then from Spotlight if this is the prompt you doubled. A name that is not on the list pays nothing. Being harder to think of is not a wildcard.

Checking runs in this browser against the published prompt files. The [privacy policy](/privacy-policy/) says that, and it says there is no account. Streak and round stats stay in localStorage on this device. Clearing site data clears them. Another browser does not have them.

## How does the daily round work?

A daily round deals up to seven prompts, and everyone on the same local date gets that set. When more than seven categories have prompts, the extras sit out and the ones that sit out rotate with the date. The round does not repeat a prompt inside itself, and it does not invent a prompt to reach seven. It resets at local midnight. The daily screen counts down to that reset.

The categories are geography, animals, food, sports, movies and TV, music, science, and history. A single category is its own page, for example [animal trivia](/trivia/animals/).

Finishing the daily counts at most once toward the streak on a given local date. Playing again on the next local day continues the streak. A longer gap starts it at 1. [Unlimited mode](/unlimited/) and a category round use the same tiers, the same clock, and the same one Spotlight. They do not move the streak.

You can turn the timer off before the round. Leaving it on is the ordinary daily. The share text says "No timer" when you used the switch, so an untimed total is not the same claim as a clocked one.

Enter submits. Tab moves between the answer box, Guess, and Pass. Escape closes a dialog. When the system asks for reduced motion, the tier animation follows that. None of that changes the points.

## What does each tier pay?

| Tier | Name | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Common | 5 |
| 2 | Familiar | 10 |
| 3 | Uncommon | 20 |
| 4 | Rare | 40 |
| 5 | Epic | 70 |
| 6 | Legendary | 100 |

These names belong to this game. They are not another site's labels.

On the published great-ape prompt, Human and Bonobo are Common, chimpanzee is Familiar, western gorilla and Bornean orangutan are Uncommon, eastern gorilla is Rare, Sumatran orangutan is Epic, and the Tapanuli orangutan is Legendary. Two names sharing a bucket is normal. The rows are on [rarest species of great ape](/answers/great-ape-species/). Other published lists work the same way. The [rarest country in Africa](/answers/countries-in-africa/), by pageviews on that list, is Togo, and Egypt, Nigeria, and South Africa are Common. The [rarest chemical element](/answers/chemical-elements/), by pageviews on that list, is Bohrium, and gold is Common. On the Record of the Year file, the [rarest Grammy winner](/answers/grammy-record-of-the-year/) by pageviews is Hans Graf, and Billie Eilish is Common. Krillion's acceptance and scores may differ.

Seven Legendary answers are 700 points. Seven Common answers are 35. Both rounds can be entirely correct. A blank is 0, which drops a chance at up to 100 rather than a small penalty.

The steps between tiers are uneven: 5, then 10, then 20, then 30, then 30. A Rare answer you can spell is already 40, eight times a correct Common answer. Leaving that Rare name to gamble on a Legendary name you cannot finish can score 0.

## What does Spotlight change?

Spotlight is a 2× boost you arm once per round. It doubles the points of one prompt, and only if that prompt scores. Tap it again to turn it off before you submit. A miss, a pass, or a timeout does not spend it, so you can arm it on a later prompt.

| Tier | Doubled |
| --- | --- |
| Common | 10 |
| Familiar | 20 |
| Uncommon | 40 |
| Rare | 80 |
| Epic | 140 |
| Legendary | 200 |

Used on a Legendary answer, that prompt is 200. If the other six prompts are also Legendary, the round is 800. Spotlight cannot double more than one prompt. Used on a Common answer, 5 becomes 10 and the only double is gone. That 10 is still less than one undoubled Rare answer.

Spotlight is not a difficulty, a currency, or a second mode. Hold it for a specific name you can explain. That choice is on [how to find a rare answer](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/).

## What do a miss, a pass, and a timeout score?

Each prompt gives you 25 seconds. A wrong guess costs one second and the box shakes. You can try a different name until the clock ends. If a miss uses the last second, the prompt ends at 0. Pass ends the prompt at 0. Running out of time ends it at 0.

With the timer off, a wrong guess only shakes. Pass still scores 0. Turning the timer off does not change the tier values.

## How is the tier chosen?

The game reads the tier stored on the answer. It does not recompute fame while you type.

The files are ranked inside one prompt. The signal is English Wikipedia pageviews, summed over the 12 complete months from November 2025 through October 2026. The interjections prompt is the exception: its source line is Wikidata lexemes plus English Wiktionary pageviews for the same window. A source line of `Wikidata + Wikipedia pageviews (2026-10)` means that window. The figure on an answer page is the sum, not an average month and not a count of what players typed.

Rarity is the inverted average-rank percentile of that sum, so 1 is the least viewed name in the prompt. Ties share a rarity. The cuts are Common below 0.2, Familiar below 0.4, Uncommon below 0.6, Rare below 0.8, Epic below 0.93, and Legendary from 0.93 up. Wikimedia's [page view reference](https://doc.wikimedia.org/generated-data-platform/aqs/analytics-api/reference/page-views.html) says those endpoints report views for Wikimedia projects, with data from 1 July 2015.

Use the stored tier. Do not re-cut the list while the clock is running.

## What does the reveal show?

After a prompt you see your tier and a six-part meter. A list of ten or fewer answers is shown in full, rarest first. A longer list shows the ten rarest and the ten most common, and says how many answers were available. A Legendary hit on eight names is a different result from a Legendary hit on a list of hundreds. The end screen adds the points and counts how many prompts landed in each tier.

The share text is colored squares, one per prompt, plus the score. It can also say "Spotlight used" and "No timer." It does not include the names you typed. You can copy that text or download an image.
