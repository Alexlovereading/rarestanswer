Guess the rarest answer is a decision, not a pile of facts you either have or lack. You will know a legal name. The room will know one too. A rare answer game pays you for a different legal name, and then for not throwing the rest of the round away while you hunt. The [point table](/guides/how-to-play-rarest-answer/) stays fixed. This page is what to do with it.

If you cannot yet produce the second name, read [finding rare answers](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/) first. Strategy on top of an empty second idea is just typing "chimpanzee" more slowly.

## How do you guess the rarest answer in a rare answer game?

Start by naming the answer you refuse to submit. That is the common tier, or close to it. Then take one step of specificity: a region, a later split, a sister species, a side character. The finding guide does this with great apes. On a show, the step is "not the face on the poster," which is why [Stranger Things characters](/guides/stranger-things-characters-trivia/) work as a drill even before a scored prompt exists.

Hold the new name to one sentence: "This is a kind of X, because..." If the sentence needs a wink, it is not an answer. If it is boring and true, submit it. Boring, true, and one level past the room is the target.

On the sample ape list the sentence is easy to audit. "The Tapanuli orangutan is an orangutan species from South Tapanuli on Sumatra" matches a real species, described in 2017, and it is the Legendary row in the sample. "A gorilla from a documentary I half remember" is not a row. Specificity you cannot name is still the common guess, only later.

Aliases matter at submit time. If the list treats Grauer's gorilla as Eastern gorilla, that name is the Epic row, not a secret tier above it. A synonym of Human does not make Human rare. The matcher accepts honest spellings. It does not promote a nickname.

## Which tier is actually worth chasing?

Points are not linear. Common is 5, Familiar 10, Uncommon 20, Rare 40, Epic 70, Legendary 100.

- Common to Familiar is only plus 5.
- Familiar to Uncommon is plus 10.
- Uncommon to Rare is plus 20.
- Rare to Epic, and Epic to Legendary, are plus 30 each.

The expensive mistake is leaving a Rare you can spell to swing at a Legendary you cannot. Rare is already 40, eight times a correct-but-common answer. If the "rarer" idea is a coin flip between Epic and a rejection, it can lose to the Rare you were holding. A sure step you can defend beats a leap you cannot.

The other mistake is banking the postcard answer because a miss would feel embarrassing. Embarrassment is not in the scoring. Five points is. On a huge set, such as cities or foods, you do not need the single rarest member on Earth. You need any true member from outside the postcard list. Chasing a mythical top answer is how you misspell and score zero.

Use the size of the set. A handful of famous members, like great apes, rewards the one textbooks added late. A vast set rewards a real member from a region you can picture. The reveal will show the size after you submit. You can usually feel it before.

## When should you spend Spotlight?

You get one Spotlight. It doubles one prompt: 10, 20, 40, 80, 140, or 200 as you climb from Common to Legendary. It does nothing for a rejected answer, and it does not change the tier. Doubling a name you were going to type anyway converts 5 into 10.

Spend it when three things are true.

1. You have a specific word, not a mood.
2. The sentence test passes.
3. You are not saving it for a later prompt you actually know better. Prompts are not ordered from easy to hard. They are seven different sets. If the animal prompt is the one you understand, the boost belongs there even when it shows up first.

Do not spend Spotlight to make a weak prompt feel less ordinary. If every remaining idea is ordinary, double the best specific guess you can spell, not the one you are least sure of. Timer off or timer on, you still get one boost. Extra thinking is not a second Spotlight.

## How should the daily round differ from unlimited practice?

The [daily round](/) is the streak: seven shared prompts, reset at local midnight. Play it like a match. Two names, sentence test, Spotlight on the prompt you understand, common name only as a fallback when the clock is dying. Do not use the daily to discover whether a wild guess is on the list. The reveal will teach you that after the prompt is already spent.

[Unlimited mode](/unlimited/) is the lab. Turn the timer off and force the second name. Then turn the timer on and see if that name still arrives before you panic back to the first. One category at a time teaches faster. Animals teach the species cut. Screen prompts teach the poster cut. Mixing them feels like a real round and teaches less per minute.

A short session is enough. A few untimed prompts, reading every common column, then one timed prompt where you may submit the second name only if the sentence test passes. Mashing famous names rehearses the habit you are trying to drop.

Play the daily first if you care about today's streak, and do the lab after. The daily set is the same for everyone that day. It is not the same set as a random unlimited prompt. A name you just saw on a reveal is not an automatic spoiler for a different question. Looking up a published answer list for today's seven is a different activity from playing.

## What do you do with the reveal, and which habits should you drop?

The reveal shows your tier, the top 10 rarest answers, the most common answers, and how many answers were on the list. Study them in this order.

Most common first. Keep the pattern, not the proper noun. If the common column is all capitals, the next geography prompt bans capitals. If it is the four leads, the next screen prompt bans the poster. The names expire with the prompt. The pattern does not.

The rare column second. Notice the shape: regional names, later historical names, sub-categories, side characters with a job. Copy the shape next time. Memorizing today's Legendary city will not name tomorrow's animal.

The size of the list third. A Legendary hit on eight answers is knowledge of a small set. A Legendary hit on a huge set is selection. Only the second one means the technique is working on the prompts that fill most rounds.

Then drop three habits that feel clever and are not.

- A longer version of the common answer. If "common chimpanzee" is an alias of the chimpanzee row, the extra word bought you nothing.
- Leaving the category to sound rare. A film about the subject is not the subject. Some puzzles in the [daily trivia roundup](/guides/best-daily-trivia-games/) want a lateral leap. This game does not.
- Spending the clock on a spelling you cannot finish. A Rare name you can submit beats an Epic name that never leaves the box.

The next prompt is a new set. Only the score carries. More than seven reps means unlimited. The shared seven live on the [homepage](/), and they reset at local midnight.
