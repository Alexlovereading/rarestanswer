Geography tips for a rarest-answer round are a habit, not a list of secret capitals. The prompt names a set of places. Every accepted member is correct. The score follows how uncommon that member is inside the set. The capital on the poster is often legal and cheap. The place you would not put on a postcard is where the points are, if it really belongs.

Start from the [homepage](/) when you want today's shared round, and open [geography trivia](/trivia/geography/) when you want only places. The move itself is the same one on the [finding guide](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/): say the obvious name silently, then refuse to type it.

## What is a geography prompt actually asking?

It is a naming game. It is not a pin on a map with one official key, and it is not "which country is this outline." A prompt can ask for a country, a capital, a river, a range, or a coast. You type one answer. If the name is on that prompt's list, it already has a tier. If it is not on the list, the prompt pays nothing.

That last rule is the one people skip when they chase rarity. Obscurity is not a wildcard. A river in the wrong continent, a neighborhood when the prompt asked for a country, or a nickname you made up is a miss. A miss is worth zero. On the clock, a wrong guess also costs one second, and you can try again until the 25 seconds run out. With the timer off, a wrong guess only shakes, and Pass ends the prompt with no points.

The [rules page](/guides/how-to-play-rarest-answer/) has the full tier table. For this page the short version is enough: Common is 5 points and Legendary is 100. A correct famous capital is a small score. A blank is a smaller one.

## How should you pick a place when many places are right?

Refuse the first name. In a room, the first names overlap, and the overlap is the common tier. Replace it with a real member that is more specific or less taught.

The published sample is not a map, but the shape is the same. On "Name a species of great ape," Human is the common row and the Tapanuli orangutan is the rarest stored tier. The unspecialized word "orangutan" is not its own row. The list splits orangutans by place: Bornean, Sumatran, and Tapanuli. A geography prompt that asks for a capital, a park, or a river rewards the same instinct. Leave the name on the poster. Name a legal member a textbook mentions once.

Do not invent the key. This guide does not assign tiers to Paris, Tokyo, the Nile, or any other place, because those tiers exist only inside a published prompt. The [answer hub](/rarest-answers/) lists the geography prompts that are on the site. Open the prompt you are about to play. The eight names on [great ape species](/answers/great-ape-species/) are a finished list, not a capital key.

Wikipedia's article on the family [Hominidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominidae) describes eight living species in four genera: the three orangutans, the eastern and western gorillas, the chimpanzee and the bonobo, and modern humans. That is a biology fact about the set. It is not a promise that every future animal or geography prompt uses eight answers.

## What if no geography list is loaded?

When the list is empty, the screen says so in the game's own words: no geography prompts are loaded yet, and the daily round still plays whatever is in the list. Play stays disabled until a prompt exists. That message is an empty catalog, not a broken page, and it is not a hint to guess countries into a void.

Use the wait for practice that does transfer. Play the sample on [animal trivia](/trivia/animals/) or in [unlimited mode](/unlimited/). Read the reveal. The common column is the list of names you should have refused. The rare column is a menu of shapes, not a list to memorize for a river tomorrow. "How many you could have named" tells you whether you just beat a list of eight or a list of hundreds. Those are different skills.

When a geography prompt does land, read the question before you get clever. "Name a country" and "name a capital" accept different nouns. A city is not a country because it is famous. A region is not a national park because you have been there. [How to find a rare answer](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/) is the in-round choice between a safe common answer and a specific one you can defend.

## Does knowing more places make the rare answer easier?

It makes more legal answers available. It does not label them. Two sites checked on 14 November 2026 are useful drills and a bad score key.

[Seterra](https://www.seterra.com/) titles itself "The Ultimate Map Quiz Site" and the visible page says "Master Geography with +400 Map Quizzes," with a free play button and an educator line for printouts. Its meta description on that same fetch repeated GeoGuessr's sentence about recognizing surroundings, so that sentence is not treated here as Seterra's own pitch. The player at [online.seterra.com/en](https://online.seterra.com/en) redirected to a GeoGuessr URL whose text says Seterra gives access to over 400 customizable quizzes on countries, capitals, flags, oceans, lakes, and more, introduced in 1997, in more than 40 languages. A correct pin on those quizzes means you knew the place. It does not mean the place is Legendary on this site.

[World Geography Games](https://www.world-geography-games.com/) says it brings quiz games for kids, students, adults, and seniors, including places and physical geography, and it calls the games free teaching resources. Again, the goal on that site is knowing the answer they asked for. Bring the extra names back here, then still refuse the famous one.

Krillion's packs page, checked the same day, lists a Geography pack under the kicker "Passport Ready": "30 expeditions · around the world in seven answers," marked free for now, in beta, with a warning that answers and scoring may have issues. That is Krillion's product. The [Krillion page](/games/krillion/) keeps their rules separate from ours.

## How does this site intend to rank a place?

The running game scores the tier stored with the answer. Published prompts whose source names Wikipedia pageviews are ranked by popularity inside that one prompt. Popularity is a sum of monthly Wikimedia pageviews over the twelve complete months ending October 2026. Wikimedia's [page view analytics](https://doc.wikimedia.org/generated-data-platform/aqs/analytics-api/reference/page-views.html) reference says those endpoints report page views for Wikimedia projects and serve data starting 1 July 2015. Rarity is the inverted average-rank percentile of that total, so the least-viewed name in the set is at the top. Ties share a rarity. Cuts are Common below 0.2, Familiar below 0.4, Uncommon below 0.6, Rare below 0.8, Epic below 0.93, and Legendary otherwise.

The game uses the tier already stored in the file. Pageviews measure how often an article is read, which is a proxy for fame, not a poll of what players typed. A place with a long Wikipedia article can outrank a place you personally find obscure. Trust the reveal on the prompt you just played.
