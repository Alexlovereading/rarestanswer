Brainer is a daily trivia game you play in the browser at [gamebunny.app/brainer](https://gamebunny.app/brainer). Gamebunny's own pages describe it as one of their originals, next to games such as Flagged and Tiler. What those pages actually publish is thinner than a full rulesheet. This guide sticks to that public text, checked on 14 November 2026, and it leaves out question counts that other websites disagree on.

## What is Brainer?

The about page is short. Inquiries go to will@gamebunny.app. The page says the game is made in the EU. It also says your game statistics, challenge progress, and settings are stored only on your device, and it offers a delete button that wipes them. There is no account step in that explanation.

The menu shows a daily game, an archive marked as part of the full version, your stats, settings, and a link back to more games. The full version is priced on that menu at 3.79 euros for lifetime access. The archive index the game fetches, `https://data.gamebunny.app/games/brainer/puzzles/archive/index.json`, listed 256 puzzles on 14 November 2026, filed by month from November 2025 through November 2026. The archive screen says new quizzes can take a few days to appear. A completed count on that screen is the viewer's own progress, not a count of how many people have finished the game. The game's own one-line description in the same app calls Brainer a televised trivia game.

Settings confirm the presentation. You can change text speed, turn animated characters on or off, and turn TV noise on or off. The labels call those characters TV characters and the noise a television screen. So the daily quiz is staged as a show on a TV, with text that appears at a speed you choose. That is as far as the official chrome goes.

Third-party directories describe a host, a weekday schedule, eight questions, six questions, or even a rare-answer ocean. They do not match each other. None of those figures appears on the about page, the menu, the settings page, or the archive note I could read. They are omitted here on purpose.

## How do you play Brainer?

Open the daily game on a day when one is available and follow the screen. Answer in whatever form that quiz asks. I cannot honestly tell you that every quiz is multiple choice, or that one of them asks you to draw, because the official pages I fetched did not say so. If the text moves too fast, lower the text speed before you start. If the animation is distracting, turn the characters off. TV noise is optional too.

Your stats stay on the device. Clearing the site data, or using the delete control on the about page, drops the history the about page lists. There is no cloud copy described there.

When you want a game with a published rules page, switch. [Rarest Answer](/) states its daily shape: seven shared prompts, a higher score for a rarer accepted answer, tiers from Common at 5 points to Legendary at 100. [Cosmillion](/games/cosmillion/) publishes a six-rank table and a 25-second clock. [Seldom](/games/seldom-game/) publishes five guesses and a lower-is-better total. Brainer's public pages do not give you that kind of table, so a strategy paragraph here would be fiction.

## Is there an unlimited mode?

The archive is the extra-play option the menu shows, and it is behind the full version at the price printed there. I did not find a free mode that keeps dealing new Brainer quizzes after the daily one. The archive note about a delay before new quizzes appear means "unlimited" would still be a library of past shows, not an infinite generator.

[Unlimited mode](/unlimited/) on this site is for Rarest Answer, not for Brainer. Use it when you want more rare-answer prompts the same day. Use Brainer's archive, if you buy the full version, when you want older Brainer quizzes. They are different purchases and different games.

## Where Brainer fits among daily games

Brainer belongs in a list of [games like Wordle](/games/games-like-wordle/) only as a daily trivia habit. It is not a five-letter grid, as far as its own pages show. It belongs in [games like Krillion](/games/games-like-krillion/) only as a neighbour. Krillion, Jinxed, Wonderhop's Abyss, and this site's game all publish a rare-answer score. Brainer's checked pages do not.

[Wonderhop](/games/wonderhop/) is the other show-like daily game in this set, and it does publish its four modes, its timers, and its price for unlimited play. [Dartle](/games/dartle-game/) publishes the checkout to 100. [The Majority game](/games/majority-game/) publishes the crowd-guessing rule. If you enjoy a hosted quiz and you are comfortable learning the question types by playing, Brainer is there. If you want to know the scoring before you start, pick one of those others, or read [how to find rare answers](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/) and play [today's round](/).

The [rarest answers](/rarest-answers/) pages will not spoil a Brainer quiz. They list accepted answers for this site's prompts. That is still a useful way to see what a rare valid answer looks like when a game is willing to show its board.

## What did the site say about access on 15 November 2026?

[gamebunny.app/brainer](https://gamebunny.app/brainer), the about page, and the menu each returned HTTP 200 with the title "Gamebunny - Play free daily games like Brainer, Flagged, and Tiler, directly in your browser." Those responses were client-rendered shells. The euro price and the archive count were not in that HTML, so they were not re-read on this date. An iPhone Safari request the same day returned HTTP 200 and the same title, and it did not redirect to an app store. A finished round was not played on a phone.
