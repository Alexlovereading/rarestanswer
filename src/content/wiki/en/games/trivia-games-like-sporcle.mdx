Trivia games like Sporcle are quiz libraries: many topics, a clock or a checklist, and a reason to start another one when the first one ends. They are not all the same format. Some want every country on a map. Some want a graded multiple-choice set. Some publish a fresh staff quiz every few days. This page only includes sites that returned a readable page on 14 November 2026, and it quotes those pages instead of a review that named them.

The rarest-answer game on this site is the odd one out, on purpose. Start it from the [homepage](/) or from [unlimited mode](/unlimited/). It is listed after the libraries so the difference is easy to see. Shared dailies that are not libraries live on [best daily trivia games](/guides/best-daily-trivia-games/).

## What did Sporcle's own pages say?

The homepage title is "Sporcle | Play Fun Trivia Quizzes on Every Topic." Visible text includes "Unlimited quizzes and puzzle games" and "Let your brain play every day," beside a Daily Dose and featured quizzes with times and difficulty labels. Categories in the navigation include Sports, Geography, Music, Movies, Television, History, Literature, Language, and Science. There is also a "For Kids" entry and a Lessons entry. This guide does not describe the contents of those kids' quizzes, because the homepage did not.

The [about page](https://www.sporcle.com/about/) says players explore quizzes "across every kind of topic, from NFL trivia and the periodic table to world capitals, TV characters, music intros, classic literature, and fast food mascots," and that daily puzzles are there "for a quick brain warm up." The periodic table named there is [chemical elements](/answers/chemical-elements/), under [science trivia](/trivia/science/). It says that every day more than a million quizzes are played online, and that more than one million community-created quizzes are on the site. Those are two sentences. This page does not collapse them into a single statistic. The [games page](https://www.sporcle.com/games/) is a separate shelf, titled as a daily place to play, with named puzzles such as Acrostic and WorldPlay and links to archives. Its copyright line reads "2007 - 2026 Sporcle, Inc." A copyright range is not a founding date, so none is inferred.

A Sporcle-like game, for the rest of this list, is a site where you can open a quiz on a topic without waiting for one shared answer. Map pins and magazine quizzes qualify only as neighbors, and they are labeled that way.

## Which other quiz libraries returned a page?

**[FunTrivia](https://www.funtrivia.com/).** The title calls it the world's largest trivia site. The homepage offers trivia questions, trivia quizzes, "Daily and Hourly Trivia Games," and an educational block for parents, teachers, and students. Topic names on the page include Animals, Geography, Movies, Music, Science, Sports, and Television. The same fetch did not settle the size. The hero line said "2.5 Million Questions | 160,000 quizzes | 14,000 Topics" and "Updated Daily, Since 1995." Another heading said "168,616 Trivia Quizzes." The [quizzes index](https://www.funtrivia.com/quizzes/) title said "155,501 Quizzes," while its description said over 2 million questions and the meta description said 155,000 quizzes. Quote the page you are looking at, or quote none of the totals. The product claim that does not depend on the argument is that you can start a topic quiz, and that the site also runs games on a daily and hourly schedule.

**[TriviaPlaza](https://www.triviaplaza.com/).** The title says you play online trivia quizzes and get graded. Categories printed on the page: Pop Music, Movie, Geography, Science, Computer, Literature, Classical Music, History, Television, Sports, Languages, Miscellaneous, and General Knowledge. Recent quizzes on that fetch were film "missing word" sets by year, through 2025. There is an About link and a disclaimer link. No sentence on the homepage described a rarity score.

**[Mental Floss quizzes](https://www.mentalfloss.com/quizzes).** The index is a dated feed of staff quizzes. Geography is one of the sections. Individual quizzes in the list are things like finishing a title, matching a character, or spotting a visual gag. That is a magazine quiz, published when an editor publishes it, not a community bank the size of Sporcle's about page and not a rarest-answer prompt. The main [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/) description says the site has history, trivia, and quizzes. Open the quiz that is linked. Do not assume yesterday's Halloween quiz is still the front door.

## Which neighbors are maps, not quiz banks?

**[Seterra](https://www.seterra.com/)** is the closest geography cousin people reach for after a Sporcle map. The visible page says "Master Geography with +400 Map Quizzes," "Play now Free!," and "Educator? Download printouts." Its meta description on that fetch was GeoGuessr's sentence about recognizing surroundings, which does not match the visible headings, so it is not used as Seterra's pitch. The URL [online.seterra.com/en](https://online.seterra.com/en) redirected to [geoguessr.com/quiz/seterra](https://www.geoguessr.com/quiz/seterra), where the text says Seterra offers over 400 customizable quizzes on countries, capitals, flags, oceans, and lakes, introduced in 1997, in more than 40 languages. You are placing or naming a location they chose. You are not being paid more for a rarer correct country.

**[World Geography Games](https://www.world-geography-games.com/)** says it brings quiz games for kids, students, adults, and seniors, and that the quizzes are free teaching resources for geography class, online school, and homeschooling. Same caveat: their question has a target. [GeoGuessr](https://www.geoguessr.com/) itself, separate from the Seterra redirect, says it challenges you to recognize your surroundings. That is a street view, not a typed list.

How a rarest-answer geography prompt differs, when one is published, is the [geography tips](/guides/rarest-answer-game-tips-geography/) page. [Geography trivia](/trivia/geography/) is the category on this site. If it says no prompts are loaded, that is the catalog, not a hidden Sporcle board.

## What did not open, and what is a different genre?

JetPunk returned HTTP 403 and a challenge page, not a quiz. Britannica's quiz browse and homepage did the same. Braingle returned 403. PurposeGames returned a human-verification page. Quizizz redirected to wayground.com and did not yield readable copy. None of those is described, ranked, or given a quiz count from another article.

Wordle-likes are the wrong shelf. They hide one word. [Games like Wordle](/games/games-like-wordle/) is their list. Krillion and this site hide nothing: many answers are right, and a rarer one scores more. Krillion's version of that idea is [their page](/games/krillion/). A library you can replay is [best unlimited trivia games](/guides/best-unlimited-trivia-games/).

If the thing you liked about Sporcle was "sit down and finish a list," stay with Sporcle, FunTrivia, or TriviaPlaza. If the thing you liked was "I know a more obscure one," the list-completion sites will not pay you for it. Neighbours that do are on [games like Krillion](/games/games-like-krillion/). Type it on the daily here, then read the reveal to see whether it was actually obscure.
