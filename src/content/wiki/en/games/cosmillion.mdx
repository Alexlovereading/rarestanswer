The Cosmillion game is a free daily word game in which the rarer correct answer scores more. You get a prompt such as "Name a fruit," you type one thing that fits, and a common pick barely moves the rocket. The how-to-play page on [cosmillion.com](https://cosmillion.com/how-to-play) is the source for the rules below. It was checked on 14 November 2026. This guide is unofficial.

## What is Cosmillion?

Everyone playing on a given day receives the same seven prompts. You have 25 seconds for each one. There is no multiple-choice board. Any answer that fits can score, and the less expected valid answer scores more. The site's own example, for "Name a fruit," puts Apple at +10 and Jabuticaba at +100. On this site the same kind of prompt is [fruits](/answers/fruits/), in [food trivia](/trivia/food/), with a different rank table.

Your total decides how high the rocket flies: through the sky, into orbit, past the Moon and Mars. The copy on the how-to-play page describes the trip as running from the ocean floor out to deep space. Seven answers at the top rank add up to 700 points. When you land, you can see how each answer ranked and send a friend a challenge link. They play the same seven prompts for free and try to beat your score.

The daily game needs no sign-up and no download. It runs in the browser on a phone or a computer.

## How do you play the Cosmillion game?

Read the whole prompt, including any extra constraint if one is on screen, and submit one answer before the 25 seconds end. Spelling variants, plurals, and accents are accepted. A close typo can come back as a "did you mean" suggestion, and you confirm it. A made-up answer that does not fit the category does not become a high score just because it is unusual. The site says guessing is not the same thing as a rare valid answer.

A new set of seven prompts goes live every day at midnight New York time. The how-to-play page also gives that as 5 am in London and 10:30 am in India.

Ranks on the official table are:

| Rank | Points | What the site says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tourist | +10 | Everyone packed that one |
| Overthought | +15 | Felt clever, and the whole crew thought of it too |
| Cadet | +30 | Solid, flying with the squadron |
| Astronaut | +60 | Genuinely uncommon |
| Deep Space | +85 | Almost nobody went there |
| One in a Galaxy | +100 | The rarest answer on the board |

Those names belong to Cosmillion. [Rarest Answer](/) uses a different ladder: Common, Familiar, Uncommon, Rare, Epic, and Legendary, worth 5, 10, 20, 40, 70, and 100. If you are comparing a screenshot with a friend, check which game it came from before you compare the numbers.

## Is there an unlimited mode?

The daily launch is free, and the site says it stays free. An optional one-time All Access purchase adds unlimited launches, the archive, and themed packs. That is the published way to play more than the day's seven prompts. Prices for All Access were not listed on the how-to-play page when this article was checked, so none are stated here.

On this site, extra rounds are the free [unlimited mode](/unlimited/). The daily set is still the one to share, because everyone else had the same prompts. Unlimited is for practice.

## What helps you score higher?

The skill is the second answer, not a faster first one. For a broad prompt, name the thing you believe is real and specific, then ask whether a crowded room would have said it too. "Overthought" exists because the clever-sounding pick is often the crowded one. A regional fruit, a technical term, or a species you could point to in a reference book is the shape of a Deep Space or One in a Galaxy answer in their fruit example. It still has to fit.

After you land, read the ranking. The value of the round is the list of answers you did not think of. That habit transfers to other rare-answer games. The [how to find rare answers](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/) guide on this site is written for that second-thought step. The [rarest answers](/rarest-answers/) pages show how this site scores its own prompts, which is useful practice even when the day's Cosmillion board is different.

## How does Cosmillion compare with similar games?

[Krillion](/games/krillion/) is the game most people mean when they search for this style. Its official FAQ describes a seven-round daily dive and a top gem called the Krillion, chosen for fun as well as rarity. It does not publish the six rank names in the table above. Treat the two games as cousins with separate answer lists.

[Seldom](/games/seldom-game/) also wants a rare valid answer, and it wants a low number. Five guesses, one question, and a 0 is the prize. Cosmillion wants a high number, one answer per prompt, seven prompts.

[Jinxed](/games/jinxed-game/) lets a room type many answers at once and splits points when friends match. Cosmillion is one answer per prompt, then an optional challenge link after you finish. [Wonderhop](/games/wonderhop/) folds a rare-answer mode, called Abyss, into a set of four daily games that also ask for the most common, the oldest, and the newest answer.

A longer list, with the games that only share the daily habit, is on [games like Krillion](/games/games-like-krillion/).
