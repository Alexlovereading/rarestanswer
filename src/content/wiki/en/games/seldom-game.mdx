The Seldom game is a daily trivia puzzle where the rarer valid answer scores lower, and lower is the win. It is free in the browser at [playseldom.com](https://www.playseldom.com/). The rules below come from the site's how-to-play and about pages, checked on 14 November 2026.

## What is Seldom?

Most trivia pays you for the answer a quiz book would print in bold. Seldom asks you to imagine showing your answer to 100 people and to count how many would say "oh yes, obviously." That count is your score for the guess. Your job is to find valid answers that would get the smallest count.

The about page says the game launched in April 2026. The reason given there is that daily games such as Wordle and Connections reward the single correct result, and the makers wanted a daily game that rewards a different valid result. Categories are everyday ones: fruit, countries, animals, movies. A fruit question here is [fruits](/answers/fruits/), under [food trivia](/trivia/food/). The site says specialist knowledge is not required.

## How do you play the Seldom game?

Every player in the world gets the same question, for example "Name a fruit." You get five guesses. Each guess scores between 0 and 100. Your total is the sum, out of 500. Lower is better.

A guess that is not on the board counts as a miss. Misses do not change the numeric score, and they do show on your results and when you share, so a clean card is visible. A typo the game recognizes, something that is only part of an answer, and a repeat of a guess you already tried do not count as misses.

The reference group of "100 people" is described as a mix from English-speaking countries. Where an answer is from matters less than where it is known. A kangaroo is Australian, and the site points out that the whole world knows it, so it scores high. Something that is everyday in one country and unheard of elsewhere scores low, because most of that reference group has never heard of it. Regional names and spellings are accepted. The site's advice is to use the name you would actually say.

Famous is not the same as obvious for the question in front of you. The how-to-play page uses chewing gum: everyone knows what it is, and for "Name a food that comes from a tree" almost nobody would think of it, so it scores 1 on that prompt.

Their worked fruit example is Apple 95, Mango 48, Kiwi 32, Lychee 5, Rambutan 0. A score of 0 on any guess is called a Seldom. Five zeros is a perfect 0/500.

You may take up to two hints. Each hint shows part of an answer. Revealing extra letters adds to your score if you then use that answer, so a game with hints can total more than 500.

Grades on the how-to-play page:

| Total | Grade |
| --- | --- |
| 0/500 | Perfect |
| 1–50 | Legendary |
| 51–150 | Brilliant |
| 151–250 | Nice |
| 251+ | Keep Going |

A new puzzle goes live at midnight UTC. A streak counts consecutive days played. If you miss a day, playing it from the archive still counts toward the streak. The daily puzzle needs no account. Older archive puzzles may ask you to sign in, and the site says signing in is free.

## Is there an unlimited mode?

The pages checked describe the daily puzzle and the archive. They do not describe a mode that deals a fresh random question as often as you want. Catching up on past days is the published way to play more than once. If you want repeat rounds of a rare-answer game with a fresh set each time, [unlimited mode](/unlimited/) on this site is [Rarest Answer](/), which scores the other direction: a higher tier is rarer.

## What helps you score lower?

The site's own tips match the scoring rule. The first answer that pops up is often an 80 or higher. A more specific member of the category tends to score lower than the household name. Local knowledge is useful because the reference group is broad: a snack or a bird that is ordinary where you live can be a blank face everywhere else, and that is a low score by the game's definition. Aliases exist, so the everyday name is allowed.

Avoid spending a guess on an answer that is merely obscure and does not fit. A miss does not add points, and it does mark the shared result.

After the puzzle, look at which of your "clever" guesses still scored high. That is the same lesson as Cosmillion's Overthought rank and Krillion's "too clever" label, expressed as a big number instead of a small one. The [how to find rare answers](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/) guide walks through that second-guess step. You can compare the shape of a finished prompt with the [rarest answers](/rarest-answers/) lists on this site.

## How does Seldom compare with similar games?

[Cosmillion](/games/cosmillion/) gives you seven prompts and one answer each, and a higher score is better, up to 100 a prompt. Seldom gives you one prompt and five answers, and a lower score is better, down to 0.

[Krillion](/games/krillion/) is a seven-round daily dive. Its FAQ describes how answer lists and the top gem are built. It does not use Seldom's 0-to-100 "how many of 100 people" scale.

[Jinxed](/games/jinxed-game/) is multiplayer: many answers, a shared clock, and split points when you match a friend. Seldom is solo, five named guesses, one question.

[Rarest Answer](/) is seven shared prompts and a higher score for the rarer accepted answer, with tiers from Common at 5 points to Legendary at 100. A wider set of daily games is on [games like Krillion](/games/games-like-krillion/). [Games like Wordle](/games/games-like-wordle/) covers the one-hidden-word habit, which Seldom's about page names as the thing it was built to sit beside.
