The Farflung game is a free daily geography quiz you answer on a map. Five prompts, one country pin each, and a less obvious correct country takes you further. The official home is [farflung.game](https://farflung.game/). Rules below come from the how-to-play and about pages, checked on 14 November 2026. The site says another domain using the name is not this game.

## What is Farflung?

Each prompt asks for a country that fits a rule. Several countries can be right. You choose one and drop a pin. After five prompts you reach camp: total distance, the other answers, and facts about the countries. You can share the result from there.

A correct answer adds distance in kilometres. Rarity labels are Common, Uncommon, Rare, Elusive, and Farflung. The Farflung label is the gem, and each prompt has at least one. The answers people are least likely to think of travel furthest. A wrong answer adds no distance.

The about page's reason for the game is the second or third country, not the first. Ask for a place where German is an official language and many people think of Germany. It counts. Luxembourg takes you much further. The how-to-play page prints that example in full and then says it is only for the guide. It does not appear in the daily game or in practice. The guide's ladder for that prompt is Germany as Common, Austria as Uncommon, Liechtenstein as Rare, Belgium as Elusive, and Luxembourg as Farflung, with the note that German is official there alongside French and Luxembourgish. The homepage FAQ shortens the same idea and names Belgium as the country that carries you further. When the two pages look like they disagree, the how-to-play ladder is the one that ranks both countries.

Farflung is made by Steve and published by Farflung LLC. Help mail is support@farflung.game. Press mail is hello@farflung.game. The homepage names the official accounts as @playfarflung on Instagram, TikTok, and X, and r/farflung on Reddit.

## How do you play the Farflung game?

Open the site and start the daily expedition. You can play as a guest. Nothing to download.

1. Read the prompt and think of a country that fits. With the clock off, take the time you need.
2. Tap the map to place a pin. Tap again to move it. Scroll or pinch to zoom.
3. Press Drop pin. You get one answer per prompt.
4. See how far that answer goes, then press Next prompt.
5. After the fifth pin, choose On to camp.

You may use the same country on more than one prompt when it truly fits each one.

The map has no country labels printed across it. By default it draws borders and shows the name of the country under your pin before you drop it. A pin at sea or on disputed ground should be moved onto a country first. A pin on a territory counts for the country it belongs to. The how-to-play page's examples are Greenland counting as Denmark, and Guam counting as the United States. Fuller edge cases are on the site's rules page.

Keyboard play is supported. Tab to the map, use the arrow keys to pan, plus and minus to zoom, and Space or Enter to drop a pin you have already placed.

Difficulty, in the menu, is how you make it harder. You can hide the country name under the pin, hide borders, or play against a 60-second clock. With the clock on, a pin sitting on a country is dropped for you when time runs out. If no pin is on land, that prompt scores no distance. If you leave while the clock is running on an open prompt, it keeps running. If it expires while you are away, that prompt scores nothing.

A new expedition opens at midnight local time. Everyone gets the same five prompts for that date. You get one try. Leave before you finish and you can return to continue, subject to the clock rule above.

## Is there an unlimited mode?

The practice round is the repeatable mode the how-to-play page describes. From the menu, open How to play and choose Practice round. On a first visit it can also sit above the play button. Practice is one prompt, with no clock. You can try it as often as you like. Nothing is kept, and it does not use up the daily expedition.

The homepage FAQ answers "Is there a Farflung unlimited mode?" with "Not yet," and says themed packs come first. Practice is one prompt, not another five-prompt expedition.

[Unlimited mode](/unlimited/) on this site is a full extra round of [Rarest Answer](/), which is open prompts rather than a map. Farflung's practice round is the official way to learn the pin without spending the day.

## What helps you travel further?

Name a country that fits, then discard the one you thought of first if you know another that also fits. The gem is defined as a surprising answer at the top rarity, not as a country that is merely small or far from where you live. A wrong pin adds nothing, so a wild guess is a wasted prompt.

Turn the helpers off only after you can place pins with the name showing. Hiding borders and playing on the clock at the same time is a different game from the default expedition. Use practice for that before you spend the daily try.

If a ruling looks wrong, the site asks you to write to support@farflung.game. The blog explains past prompts and the geography behind them. The changelog lists feature changes by date.

For the naming skill without a map, read [how to find rare answers](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/) and compare a finished prompt with the [rarest answers](/rarest-answers/) on this site. [Krillion](/games/krillion/) and [Cosmillion](/games/cosmillion/) are typed-answer versions of the same "less obvious member of the set" idea. [Dartle](/games/dartle-game/) sometimes uses countries too, and it wants their numbers to add to 100. A wider list is on [games like Krillion](/games/games-like-krillion/).
