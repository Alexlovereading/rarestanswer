Krillion is a daily trivia dive in the browser at [krillion.io](https://krillion.io/). A less familiar valid answer is meant to outscore the obvious one. This unofficial guide was checked against that site on 14 November 2026. Other dailies that are not this game are on [games like Krillion](/games/games-like-krillion/).

## What is Krillion?

The homepage calls it "the daily dive." The menu lists Daily dive, Unlimited, Puffer Party, Archive, and Themed packs. The line under the menu is "one in a krillion." The packs page titles the product "the daily dive" and says: "Seven prompts a day, same for everyone. Rare answers sink you deeper. Obvious ones barely count."

The privacy policy, effective 23 September 2026, says you can play without an account, a name, or an email. A random id in the browser tells players apart. A finished dive stores the game, the score, and that id, which powers the "better than X% of divers" line. Guest answers on the daily are checked and then discarded. Answers on the free practice dive are kept with that id so scoring can be tuned. The policy says the game is not directed at children under 13. It also describes a Friends feature. This guide does not add rules for that feature beyond the policy's mention.

A Discord recap in the same policy can include a seven-round dive graph. That is the clearest official statement of the length: seven rounds. The FAQ does not publish a per-round clock for the daily dive. The timer it does explain belongs to Puffer Party. Fan sites quote a 25-second daily clock and a six-tier point table. Those figures are not on the FAQ, the privacy policy, the packs page, or the Puffer Party page, so they are left out here.

The FAQ says one person makes the game, posting as kp, and describes himself as a 23-year-old American computer-science graduate. The same answer says other people have claimed to be the creator, and that search has listed someone who made a copy. Business mail is business@krillion.io and support is support@krillion.io. Merch is at shop.krillion.io. The menu links [Chainle](https://chainle.io/), described there as a word-association game by the creator's younger brother.

## How do you play?

Open [krillion.io](https://krillion.io/) and start the daily dive. The FAQ says an app is planned, not shipped. Guest progress, sound, a colorblind mode, your character, and purchase unlock tokens live in the browser. Clearing site data clears them. A Google account is optional and syncs completed games to other devices.

Type an answer that fits. The server checks it. If a fair answer is missing, the FAQ says a source was missed. If it is not accepted, it did not score. Autocorrect is generous because some prompts have thousands of answers, many with aliases. The published example is Threebanded Sheephead, aliased as "sea donkey," one letter from "sea monkey," so a near-miss can snap to a creature you did not mean. Look at the answer that was accepted before you treat that snap as the gem you intended. Type the full name when a short name is one letter from a joke.

The Discord Activity is a separate save. It uses your Discord user id and display name for the dive, answers, score, history, and streak. Channel standings and recaps are optional. A recap can show a name, a score, and the seven-round graph. It does not include the answers.

## How does a rare answer get its score?

Answer sets are aggregated from dictionaries, wiki pages, encyclopedias, and whatever else the question needs, then merged and cleaned. Some prompts are scored against a familiarity database of how often a word or name shows up in what people read and write. Others get language-model help plus a manual pass.

The Krillion is the gem, and it is not chosen on rarity alone. Past a certain depth, the FAQ says, rarity gets hard to separate, and some collected answers are so obscure that almost nobody would type them. The gem is also picked because it is satisfying to land, and because at least a few players might actually enter it. That is why the Krillion is sometimes less rare than some deep cuts. "Too clever" is the go-to obscure pick that is not actually obscure. If the rare name you reach for is always the same catchphrase, other players are reaching for it too. Hunting the most invisible legal string can miss the gem on purpose. The target the FAQ describes sits between those misses.

## How does the game on this site differ?

[Rarest Answer](/) is a different product. This site is not affiliated with Krillion. A score on one is not a score on the other.

Krillion sends each answer you type to its server to be checked. This site checks guesses in the browser against published prompt data. Streak and round stats stay in localStorage on the device. They are not uploaded to a Rarest Answer server. Optional Google sign-in on Krillion can sync completed games. This site does not offer an account. The [privacy policy](/privacy-policy/) says this game is not directed at children under 13 either. Optional ads, analytics, and comments load only when their environment values are set, and they stay outside the play card.

A daily round here deals up to seven prompts, the same set for everyone on your local date, and fewer than seven when the catalog cannot fill seven. Six tiers run from Common at 5 points to Legendary at 100. One Spotlight boost doubles a single scored prompt. The [rules](/guides/how-to-play-rarest-answer/) are the long version, including the point table. The builder ranks answers inside one prompt by Wikimedia pageviews and stores a tier. The running game scores that stored tier. It does not add a fun factor on top. The great-ape list's source is Wikidata plus Wikipedia pageviews (2026-10). The names and stored tiers are on [great ape species](/answers/great-ape-species/). A method for producing a less famous legal name on this site is the [finding guide](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/).

Krillion's FAQ does not publish the 5-to-100 table. A gem there can be less rare than a deeper cut on purpose. A Legendary row here is the top stored bucket on that prompt, not a promise that it is the most endangered, the most clever, or the most fun. Neither description is a license to paste the other game's answer key.

Play Krillion if you want their dive, their gem, their packs, their archive, or Puffer Party, and if you are willing to send each guess to their server. Play here if you want the published point table, a round that stays on this device, and [unlimited](/unlimited/) or category practice that does not touch the daily streak. The Roblox experience with a similar slogan is a third product, covered on its [own page](/guides/rarest-answer-party-roblox/).

## Is there an unlimited mode?

There is a path at [krillion.io/unlimited/classic](https://krillion.io/unlimited/classic). The page still titles itself as the daily dive, and it did not expose a written rules sheet. The privacy policy separately describes a free practice dive. This guide does not claim those are the same mode, and it does not state a price. Do not assume a casual extra round is private, and do not assume it is scored like the daily, because neither claim is on that Unlimited URL.

The packs page showed At the Movies and Music when this guide was checked. The same page says themed packs are "Free for now · BETA," warns that answers and scoring may have issues, and lists Words and Language (30 chapters), Movies (30 pictures), Sports (30 games), and Geography (30 expeditions). Music is marked coming soon. Any unlock, for any pack, turns ads off across the site. Ads are handled by Mediavine. A beta pack is a poor place to memorize a gem, because the page says scoring may be wrong. The FAQ says the current focus is answer-set curation and rarity selection. More packs are planned.

Archive is described there as "Every aired daily, resurfaceable." Replaying a dive you already saw is a way to study a gem you missed. It is not the same skill as meeting a new prompt, and a remembered gem is not tomorrow's answer.

[Unlimited mode](/unlimited/) on this site deals a new set without waiting for local midnight, uses the same tiers and the same 25-second clock, and does not move the daily streak. You can turn the timer off. You can also play one category. Finished prompts are listed under [rarest answers](/rarest-answers/).

## What is Puffer Party?

[Puffer Party](https://krillion.io/party) is labeled "Krillion's multiplayer category game." You name an answer and pass the puffer before it pops, with friends or in a public room. The last player still floating wins.

The hidden fuse is random on purpose, between 10 and 30 seconds under the default settings, and the timer is shared. Pass with 20 seconds left and the next person inherits them. If fewer than 8 seconds remain, it goes back to 8. After a pop, a new fuse starts. The maker cites hot potato and JKLM's BombParty. Lag can make the puffer look alive after the turn has already moved. That room rewards a fast legal name, which is often the common one. It is the wrong place to practice the gem. The FAQ still says a fuller Krillion multiplayer, and a party game based on Krillion, are planned. Puffer Party is the multiplayer category game already public. This site has no room and no pass-the-object timer. After a round you can copy a share grid of colored squares and a score. The grid does not include the answers.

## What do the other names refer to?

The site spells the name Krillion. Krillon, and Krillian, are spellings people type for that same game. A lookalike domain is a different product.

Noillirk is the name reversed. On 14 November 2026, [playdaily.org/noillirk](https://playdaily.org/noillirk) described a free survey game: 25 seconds, a Classic or Daily climb of seven prompts, and a higher score for the more common answer. That page calls itself the only official site. The same day, Apple's App Store listed an iPhone app, "Noillirk - Reverse Krillion," from BlueMind Software, and that listing also says the more common answer scores higher, across seven prompts. The two products disagree about which of them is the only one. Neither listing is krillion.io.

The [One in a Million](/games/one-in-a-million-game/) search did not find a current daily game under that title. That idiom, and the 1967 show that used it, are a different subject from this dive.
