The Majority game at [majoritygame.com](https://www.majoritygame.com/) asks you to guess what everyone else guessed. The clever answer is the wrong instinct. The popular answer is the point. Rules below come from that homepage, checked on 14 November 2026. The site does not name a studio on the page I could read, so this guide does not borrow a creator credit from a directory.

## What is the Majority game?

The homepage states one rule: think like everyone else. Its sample prompt is "What country comes to mind FIRST when you hear Africa?" That sample is a crowd guess, not the list of [countries in Africa](/answers/countries-in-africa/) in [geography trivia](/trivia/geography/). You get five fresh questions every day. They are described as quick, simple, and deceptively tricky. The instruction under the first step is explicit: not the cleverest answer, the popular one.

You type what you think most people typed. If you match the majority, the page says the crowd lights up with you. One example on the page shows a 61% match. The site frames this as a daily workout in predicting other people's favorites, and it names the Keynesian beauty contest: you win by predicting everyone else's favorite, rather than by picking your own. It also quotes the idea that the average of everyone's guess can be smarter than almost any single guess. Those are the site's words for why a typical answer beats a private one.

A line on the page says a new deck arrives at midnight. The time zone is not printed next to that line, so this guide does not assign one.

## How do you play the Majority game?

Open the site and play the day's five. Read the prompt all the way through. Wording counts. The homepage example is the Africa prompt, and the step under it says to type the popular answer, not the cleverest one. The crowd writes the short, ordinary phrase, not the essay.

Submit one answer per prompt. Then read the room. The value of the round is seeing whether your guess was the common one. There is one score to brag about across the five.

You can also write five questions of your own and get one shareable link. Friends play whenever they want. The site says this needs no accounts and no live room, and that the crowd grows with every answer. There is a place to suggest a question for the daily game. The page says the best suggestions end up in a daily deck.

## Is there an unlimited mode?

The homepage describes the daily five and the custom five-question link. It does not describe a button that deals endless random prompts. Writing your own set is the way the site gives you more than today's deck. Friends can answer that set later, which is asynchronous, not a live lobby.

[Rarest Answer](/) is the mirror image, and it does have a free [unlimited mode](/unlimited/). On a Rarest Answer prompt, the highest pageview total is the low tier, Common, at 5 points. The least-viewed accepted name can reach Legendary, at 100. That rank is Wikipedia or Wiktionary pageviews inside one prompt, not a count of guesses. Majority wants the crowd's answer. If you play both in one morning, switch on purpose. The [how to find rare answers](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/) guide will make you worse at Majority if you follow it mid-round. Use it for [today's Rarest Answer round](/), and for Majority type the answer the page is asking the crowd for.

## What helps you match the crowd?

Answer as the room, not as an expert. The site's Africa prompt is a good test. A less famous country can be a better geography answer and still lose, because the game is not asking for a better geography answer. Everyday prompts work the same way. The phrase people actually say out loud beats the phrase you would be proud of.

Play the custom link with people you know when you want a smaller, weirder crowd. The daily deck is the comparable score, because those five questions are the shared ones. A custom link's majority is only the people who opened that link.

[Wonderhop](/games/wonderhop/) Sky mode is the closest published cousin: seven questions aimed at the most common answer, inside a set that also has a rare-answer mode called Abyss. Majority is only the common-answer game. [Seldom](/games/seldom-game/) and [Cosmillion](/games/cosmillion/) pay you to leave the crowd. [Jinxed](/games/jinxed-game/) splits points when you match, so matching is a penalty there and a target here. The map of those differences is on [games like Krillion](/games/games-like-krillion/).

## Which Majority is which?

A search also returns [majority.wtf](https://majority.wtf/), titled as a game about guessing what the crowd said. Fetching it on 14 November 2026 returned an error, and a later check the same day returned HTTP 525, so its rules, reset time, and scoring are not described here. Play the daily five at majoritygame.com if you want the game this page documents. Do not mix a screenshot from another domain into that score.

[Games like Wordle](/games/games-like-wordle/) covers the one-hidden-answer daily habit. Majority hides the distribution, not a word. After you have matched the crowd, the [rarest answers](/rarest-answers/) on this site show the other end of a prepared list: the names with the fewest pageviews on that prompt.
