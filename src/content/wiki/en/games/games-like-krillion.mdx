These daily games pay you for a valid answer other people would skip. The obvious name still counts, and it scores less. This page is a map of the ones I could open and verify on 14 November 2026, with [Rarest Answer](/) included as one option rather than the only one.

## Which games share that idea?

Krillion, at [krillion.io](https://krillion.io/), calls itself the daily dive. Its privacy policy describes a seven-round dive, and its FAQ explains that answer lists are built from dictionaries, wiki pages, and encyclopedias, then scored either against a familiarity database or with a manual pass. The top gem is called the Krillion, and the FAQ says it is picked for fun as well as rarity. How that dive works, and how it differs from this site, is on the [Krillion](/games/krillion/) page. Several other sites use the name. If the domain is not krillion.io, assume it is a different game.

[Rarest Answer](/) is the game this site is built around. A daily round deals up to seven prompts, the same set on your local date, and it resets at local midnight. A less-viewed accepted answer scores more. The ladder on this site is Common (5), Familiar (10), Uncommon (20), Rare (40), Epic (70), and Legendary (100), from Wikipedia or Wiktionary pageviews inside that prompt. You can keep going in [unlimited mode](/unlimited/), play a single category such as [animal trivia](/trivia/animals/), turn the timer off, and use one Spotlight — a 2× boost — once in a round. After you play, the [rarest answers](/rarest-answers/) pages list what was accepted. It does not use Krillion's ocean theme, shrimp, or tier nicknames. If you want that game, play it on its own site.

## Which games also pay for a rare answer?

[Cosmillion](/games/cosmillion/) is the closest match in shape. Its how-to-play page says everyone gets the same seven prompts, 25 seconds each, and a rarer answer scores more, from 10 points up to 100. Seven perfect answers total 700. The daily game is free with no account. A one-time All Access purchase adds unlimited launches, an archive, and themed packs. New prompts go live at midnight New York time. The picture is a rocket, not a dive.

[Seldom](/games/seldom-game/) flips the number line. You get one shared question and five guesses. Each guess scores from 0 to 100, a lower number is better, and a 0 is what the game calls a Seldom. The about page says it launched in April 2026, with a new puzzle at midnight UTC. Hints exist, and they can push a total above 500. There is an archive. Older puzzles may ask for a free account.

[Jinxed](/games/jinxed-game/) is built for a room. Everyone types as many fitting answers as they can, rarer ones brew stronger potions (1,000 to 8,000 points), and a shared answer is split. The Daily Brew is three prompts you play alone. The site says that after that you can keep playing for free. Rooms use a four-letter code and hold up to 20 people.

[Wonderhop](/games/wonderhop/) is four games, not one. Abyss is the rare-answer mode: 40 seconds a question, seven questions, rarer is better. Sky wants the most common answer. Past and Future want the oldest or the newest. It launched publicly on 13 October 2026, from Montreal, in nine languages. The four daily games are free. A Hop Pass adds unlimited play and an archive.

[Farflung](/games/farflung-game/) keeps the rare-answer idea and puts it on a map. Five prompts, one country pin each, and a less obvious correct country travels further. Rarities run from Common to a top gem called Farflung. There is no clock unless you turn on a 60-second one. A one-prompt practice round can be repeated. It is made by Steve and published by Farflung LLC.

[Ball Knowledge](/games/ball-knowledge-game/) is football, in the sense of soccer. Its Deep Cuts mode gives you one question and five shots. A correct player becomes a card from 0 to 100, and the player fewer people name scores more. The rest of the site is a large set of other daily football puzzles, from lineups to transfer prices. It is free, and you do not need an account to play.

## Which daily games are only neighbours?

These show up in the same searches. The task they set is different, which is worth knowing before you open one expecting a rare-answer score.

[Dartle](/games/dartle-game/) is a darts checkout. One category a day, up to three answers, and the numbers have to add to exactly 100. Going over is a bust. It is free in the browser, with a new category at local midnight. Rarity is not the score.

The [Size It Up game](/games/size-it-up-game/) at gamesizeitup.com asks you to resize a purple silhouette against a green object of known size. Five rounds, up to 500 points, no timer. A separate site, sizedle.com, also uses the name Size It Up. Check the domain before you compare scores with a friend.

The [Majority game](/games/majority-game/) at majoritygame.com wants the popular answer on purpose. Five questions a day, a new deck at midnight, and a match with the crowd is the win. Wonderhop's Sky mode is the same instinct inside a larger set.

[Games like Wordle](/games/games-like-wordle/) share the ritual more than the rules. Wordle, now on The New York Times site, gives you six tries at one five-letter word. There is no rarity ladder. It is the reason so many of these games exist as a morning habit, and it is a poor substitute if what you wanted was an open "name a…" prompt.

[Brainer](/games/brainer/) is Gamebunny's browser trivia show. The official pages confirm a daily game, a TV-style screen, and an archive behind a paid full version. They do not publish a rules sheet, so this guide does not invent question counts or a scoring system for it.

## What about Deep Dive and One in a Million?

Both names are searched next to Krillion. Neither resolved to a single official rare-answer game I could verify.

The [Deep Dive game](/games/deep-dive-game/) label is shared by a static trivia page titled Deep Dive Trivia, an older arcade diver about oxygen and coins, and loose uses of "deep dive" or "deep cut" inside other games. Krillion's own FAQ uses "deep cuts" as a way to talk about obscure answers. That is still Krillion.

The [One in a Million game](/games/one-in-a-million-game/) search mostly finds the English idiom and a 1967 ABC game show. I did not find a current daily browser game under that exact title. Cosmillion's top rank is named One in a Galaxy. Krillion's FAQ calls its top gem the Krillion.

## How do you choose one?

Pick the constraint you enjoy. If you want one answer, a short clock, and a higher score for the stranger valid name, play [today's round](/) here, or Cosmillion, or Krillion on its own site. If you want five tries at one question and a score that falls as you get rarer, play Seldom. If you want friends on the same prompt at the same time, play Jinxed. If you want a map, play Farflung. If you want football only, play Ball Knowledge.

For a method that travels between these games, read [how to find rare answers](/guides/how-to-find-rare-answers/). Then check a finished prompt against the [rarest answers](/rarest-answers/) list so the next round starts from what was actually accepted, not from a guess about the crowd.
